==============================================================================
A. THE COMPARISON, AND THE RULE DECLARED IN ADVANCE
==============================================================================

  retrieval arm    `cited` prompt, BM25 over sections at depth 15
  full-corpus arm  `cited` prompt, all 45 articles, no retrieval

  Same prompt, same generator, same judge, same 120 questions. The only
  thing that differs is what the model is given to read.

  THE RULE. Sending the full corpus REPLACES retrieval only if all three
  hold:

    1. it refuses at least as many of the 25 unanswerable questions
    2. it is more correct on the 95 answerable ones, with a paired
       interval that excludes zero
    3. on questions both arms answer, its faithfulness is not lower by
       more than 0.05 (the interval's lower end stays above -0.05)

  Otherwise retrieval stays. The rule is asymmetric on purpose: the full
  corpus is the simpler system, so it wins ties on engineering grounds,
  but it has to clear safety first and it costs more per query.

==============================================================================
B. WHAT SENDING EVERYTHING COSTS  (live)
==============================================================================

                      context tokens   per 1,000 queries
  retrieval                      571               $0.15
  full corpus                  7,611               $1.21

  The full corpus is 13.3x the context. At this size that is still
  about a dollar per thousand queries, so cost alone does not rule it
  out. What cost does do is scale: every article added to the help
  centre is paid for on every query, while retrieval's context stays the
  size of its depth. At 45 articles the gap is 13x; at ten times the
  corpus it is 133x.

  Token counts are estimates (words x 1.3); prices are list prices for
  the default generator and will drift. The figure is for comparing the
  two arms, not for budgeting.

D. VERDICT
==============================================================================

  The rule from Section A, applied:

    1. refuses at least as many unanswerable questions   yes
    2. more correct on answerable ones, interval > 0     NO
    3. faithfulness not lower by more than 0.05          yes

>>> RETRIEVAL STAYS.

  The full corpus does not clear the rule: more correct in scope.
  On correctness the two cannot be told apart at this sample size.
  Retrieval stays because the challenger did not win, not because it
  lost: the case for retrieval at this corpus size is cost and
  headroom (Section B), not answer quality.

  This is a result about one comparison on 120 questions, one generator and
  one judge, stated with the intervals above rather than as a ranking.
